# Founder clarification: free-to-consumer, partner-funded marketplace

Date: 2026-11-21

This clarification supersedes the paid consumer/research-service framing in the first verdict. The founder's intended model is a public real-estate discovery and intelligence platform. People searching for properties, land and basic location information should not pay to browse or search. Revenue should come from businesses that want to reach those users or use professional tools.

## Intended sides of the marketplace

**Free public side**
- Search and browse houses, apartments, rentals, plots, land and developments.
- View property and parcel location, basic land and zoning context, and relevant neighbourhood information.
- Compare listings and discover projects.
- Contact a listing party or request a partner connection without paying Taiseel for access to search.
- Basic public market information and data discovery remain accessible.

**Paying business side**
- Agents and agencies: qualified buyer/renter enquiries, advertising share of voice, professional profiles and tools.
- Landlords and property managers: rental portfolio advertising, featured inventory, enquiry management and other professional tools.
- Developers: project showcase pages, new-construction campaigns, qualified project enquiries and market analytics.
- Banks and mortgage providers: consent-based mortgage enquiries, home-loan calculator and comparison placements, qualified application leads or agreed referral arrangements.
- Insurers, valuers, surveyors, lawyers, architects and other property professionals: verified profiles, directory placements and relevant service enquiries.
- Institutional customers: business intelligence, aggregate analytics, APIs or workflow tools under separate licences.

## How the money should work

The searcher is not the paying customer. The business customer pays because Taiseel can deliver relevant exposure, a qualified enquiry, an accepted introduction, a completed transaction under an agreed lawful model, or a professional workflow.

Potential mechanisms:
1. Prepaid partner advertising by location/category and share of voice.
2. Clearly labelled sponsored project or listing showcases.
3. Qualified-lead fees or pay-on-completion arrangements where suitable and lawful.
4. Monthly subscriptions for professional inventory, CRM, portfolio and analytics tools.
5. Mortgage and other service referrals under written partner terms, user consent and legal review.
6. Paid B2B data/API products for institutional use. This does not put a paywall on normal public search.

Do not make all partners pay the same way. A bank, developer, broker and landlord receive different kinds of value.

## Rules that protect trust

- Browsing and basic search stay free.
- Paid placement must be visibly labelled. Payment must not secretly change factual data, zoning, parcel boundaries, estimates or organic relevance.
- A basic listing can be free during the supply-building stage. Do not charge for listing volume before Taiseel can prove reach and enquiries.
- Share a seeker's contact details with a bank, agent or other partner only after clear consent for that introduction.
- A lead must be defined and measurable. Track duplicate, invalid, unanswered and converted leads.
- Keep asking prices, verified transaction data, modelled estimates and official reference values clearly separate.
- Give every listing an update date and a status. Remove stale or unavailable inventory.
- If Taiseel also develops or markets its own projects, label affiliated inventory and keep ranking rules transparent. The marketplace must not covertly favour its own projects.

## Strategy implication

The four pillars remain a valid long-term architecture:
- Land Intelligence
- Development Intelligence
- Real Estate Exchange / MLS
- Market Intelligence

They should not all be commercialised at once. Build one trusted public discovery loop and dense, current inventory first. Add paid partner products when there is measurable consumer attention and lead quality. Add mortgages and institutional data products after consent, partner integrations and demand are proven.

## Zillow evidence

Zillow's 2025 revenue was $2.583 billion: Residential $1.704 billion (66%), Mortgages $199 million (8%), Rentals $630 million (24%), Other $50 million (2%). Its stated revenue drivers include agent and seller offerings, new construction, mortgage loan origination and marketplace fees, paid rental ads, multifamily subscriptions, application fees, and display advertising. Zillow's Premier Agent model includes prepaid share-of-voice advertising and a pay-for-performance option where a partner pays when a transaction closes with a supplied lead.

This validates the revenue mechanisms, not the transferability of Zillow's US-scale traffic or economics to Rwanda.

## Rwanda market check

The free-search and paid-partner model already exists locally in some form. Kivu Listing says buyers browse and contact sellers free while sellers pay for advertising plans and optional promoted placement. Ibyungura identifies paid featured listings. Other local portals promote free consumer search and direct contacts. Therefore, "free to browse" alone will not differentiate Taiseel.

Taiseel needs a stronger reason to visit and return: reliable parcel and administrative mapping, useful zoning context, accurate and current listings, transparent price signals, verified partners and better discovery across land, projects and properties.

Potential bank partners already have relevant products. BPR advertises home loans for home purchases, construction and plots, including a loan term up to 25 years and financing up to 70% for plot purchases. I&M advertises mortgage loans, construction loans and home equity products. This shows partner fit, not an existing agreement or guaranteed willingness to pay.

## Council brief correction

The former recommendation to make paid site assessments the core business is withdrawn as the primary model. A manual assessment may be useful internally for partner onboarding, listing verification or data collection, but it is not the consumer-facing business model the founder intends.
