# Revised Round 1: opening positions

## Bezos

STANCE: Conditional yes. DOOR: two-way.
OPTION SCORES: A 6 · B 8 · C 4 · D 2
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10. CONFIDENCE: 4/5. RUIN RISK: none.
THE CALL: Choose B. Free public discovery is a durable customer need; monetise the professional ecosystem around it.
WHY:
1. Start with what the consumer will always want: relevant homes, land, location and trustworthy information (What's not going to change, S029).
2. Trust is the product. Do not sell ranking in a way that makes the marketplace unreliable.
3. B is reversible. Build inventory and measure conversion before expanding.
RISK: A large empty marketplace that partners will not pay to access.
WHAT CHANGES MY MIND: Repeat partner spend tied to measurable, qualified connections.
THIS WEEK: Define the first local inventory category and its verification standard.
FOUNDER QUESTION: What specific information makes a seeker choose Taiseel over every other portal?

## Graham

STANCE: Strong yes. DOOR: two-way.
OPTION SCORES: A 5 · B 9 · C 3 · D 2
PILLAR SCORE: 9/10. CONFIDENCE: 4/5. RUIN RISK: none.
THE CALL: Choose B. Make the free consumer product genuinely useful to a small market before trying to be everywhere.
WHY:
1. The product must create repeated consumer use, not just a partner sales story (Make Something People Want, S092).
2. A marketplace needs both sides. Manually recruit the first supply and learn what seekers actually search for (Do Things That Don't Scale, S096).
3. Four pillars at once create too many variables before one loop works.
RISK: Building supply that is not current or demand that does not convert.
WHAT CHANGES MY MIND: Weekly growth in qualified searches and contacts in one target market.
THIS WEEK: Watch real users search and record where they fail.
FOUNDER QUESTION: Which seekers return, and what brings them back?

## Hormozi

STANCE: Strong yes. DOOR: two-way.
OPTION SCORES: A 6 · B 9 · C 5 · D 2
PILLAR SCORE: 9/10. CONFIDENCE: 4/5. RUIN RISK: none.
THE CALL: Choose B. Sell partner outcomes, not a generic banner ad.
WHY:
1. The paying customer is the business partner. The offer is qualified demand, not access to a dashboard.
2. Price based on value: prepaid territory/visibility, qualified lead or closed transaction, and professional subscription are different offers (Value Equation, S115; Money Model, S113).

THIS WEEK: Interview ten agents, developers and landlords about the enquiries they would pay for.
FOUNDER QUESTION: What makes an enquiry worth paying for?

## Naval

STANCE: Strong yes. DOOR: two-way.
OPTION SCORES: A 7 · B 9 · C 5 · D 3
PILLAR SCORE: 9/10. CONFIDENCE: 4/5. RUIN RISK: none.
THE CALL: Choose B. Build the public network and own the software and permissioned data that make the network more useful over time.
WHY:

## Chair notes

The opening positions favour B. The unresolved questions are: what minimum inventory density earns repeat visits; whether partners pay for leads, advertising or subscriptions first; and how Taiseel can keep paid placement from damaging trust. The council also flags a potential conflict if Taiseel's development activity is promoted inside a marketplace it controls.
