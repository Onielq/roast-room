# Round 1: opening positions

## Bezos

SEAT: Bezos (Strategy and decisions)
STANCE: Conditional yes
OPTION SCORES: A 4/10 · B 8/10 · C 7/10 · D 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10 (Strategic fit and reversibility)
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Choose B, but let a tightly capped manual pilot prove the buyer and use case before building the paid product.
WHY:
1. Developers will keep needing trustworthy site, access, land-use and market information. Start from that durable customer need, not the four products we want to build (What's not going to change, S029; Work backwards, S056).
2. B creates a reusable product and data asset. A spreads effort across four unproven engines.
3. C is a useful two-way-door experiment, but it must be a route to a product, not an excuse to become a custom consultancy.
THE RISK I SEE: Mistaking a long-term vision for permission to spend before customers prove value.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Five or more paying developers repeatedly buying the same site-intelligence workflow.
WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Interview five developers who recently assessed or rejected a site.
QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Which buyer has already lost money or time because this information was missing?

## Graham

CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RUIN RISK: none

THE CALL: Choose C. Do the work manually for a small group before automating it.
WHY:
1. We have evidence of building, not evidence of people wanting this enough to pay. The destination is making something people want (Make something people want, S092).

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Ask ten developers to bring one live site decision and offer a paid assessment.
QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Who is already asking you for this information?

## Chair notes

**Cruxes**
1. **Sell a service first or build a software product first?** Graham, Hormozi, Martell and Buffett favour C as the fastest paid learning loop. Bezos, Gary V, Wickman and Naval favour B because software, brand and reusable data can compound.
2. **Who is the first customer?** The council is leaning toward real-estate developers making site-acquisition decisions, but this is a hypothesis, not validated demand.
3. **What is the moat?** Public parcel and planning data alone may not be exclusive. The proposed advantage would need to come from reliable interpretation, verified updates, workflow, history and distribution.
4. **Unanimity check:** Not unanimous. The strongest alternative to C is B, argued most strongly by Naval and Bezos. Graham and Hormozi must test whether the service can turn into a product rather than become labour-intensive consulting.

No seat has evidence of actual customer demand, recurring revenue or delivery margins. Do not treat the website's 120+ completed-projects statement as verified traction.
