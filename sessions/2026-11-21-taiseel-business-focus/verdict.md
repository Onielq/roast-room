## Why the council landed here

- No reviewed evidence establishes paid demand, recurring revenue or unit economics. Shipping more code will not answer those questions (Graham, Make Something People Want, S092; Hormozi, Offer First, S114).
- A real developer site decision is narrow enough to sell manually and expensive enough to test whether information creates value. Paid work will expose the repeated workflow before it is automated (Graham, Do Things That Don't Scale, S096).
- Four pillars combine different buyers, sales cycles and operating models. The first 90 days need one buyer, one offer and one owner (Hormozi, One Product / One Avatar / One Channel, S059; Wickman, Rocks and Scorecard, S025).
- Land-data access alone is not a moat. Taiseel must earn trust through source provenance, current information, interpretation, quality control and a useful workflow (Bezos, Work Backwards, S056; Buffett, Economic Moat, S080).
- The manual service is a test, not the end state. If five paid reports reveal a repeated workflow and permissioned data asset, build only that product (Naval, Three Forms of Leverage, S001).

## Where they fought

**Service first or software first?** Graham, Hormozi, Martell and Buffett favoured C from the start. Bezos, Gary V and Wickman moved from B to C after accepting that a paid pilot is reversible and produces better customer evidence. Naval remained on B because manual work can become a labour trap.

**What is the moat?** The council rejected the idea that a map interface or public parcel dataset is automatically defensible. The possible moat is reliable, time-stamped and permissioned data, accumulated history, high-quality interpretation and a repeatable decision workflow. This remains a hypothesis to prove.

**One business or two?** The development/JV proposition and the software/intelligence product must have separate economics, contractual obligations and capital decisions. Shared branding is not a reason to blend risk.

## Minority report

Naval dissents. His concern is that paid reports could make Taiseel a research desk that sells founder time without producing an owned asset. For him to be right, the pilot would need to fail to produce reusable, permissioned data or a repeated workflow. The 90-day productisation gate is intended to test that concern.

## Conditions

1. Complete 20 qualified conversations with Kigali developers or acquisition leads within 30 days.
2. Secure at least five paid assessments within 90 days. Free work does not count.
3. At least two clients must place a second order, request a retainer, or refer a paying client.
4. Track labour and direct costs. By the fifth report, target direct delivery cost below 50% of collected fees.
5. Every material claim must show source, date and confidence. Never market a site report as title verification, legal due diligence, valuation, survey or approval.
6. No new hires, borrowing, personal guarantees, speculative land purchases or fixed-cost expansion during the test.

## What to do next

1. **By 2026-11-28:** define the report, data-source register, exclusions, deposit terms and sample output.
2. **By 2026-12-21:** complete 20 buyer conversations and secure paid pilot work.
3. **By 2027-02-19:** review five paid reports, repeat demand, delivery margin and the decision to productise.

**Kill criteria:** Stop or reshape if fewer than three of 20 qualified buyers pay, if the assessment does not change a real decision, if delivery costs remain above 70% of fees after five reports, or if reliable and lawful data cannot be secured.

**Revisit:** 2027-02-19. Bring paid customer count, repeat/referral count, fees collected, direct delivery hours and costs, correction rate, report turnaround, lead source and actual cash/runway.

## Market evidence reviewed

- Rwanda's RDB describes real estate as a major investment sector and cites urbanisation and housing needs. These are market context, not proof of demand for Taiseel's product: https://rdb.rw/investment-opportunities/real-estates/
- CAHF's Rwanda country profile estimates Kigali housing demand at about 22,000 units a year and cites around 3,000 units supplied annually over the decade to 2023. These are sector estimates, not a direct addressable market for site-intelligence services: https://housingfinanceafrica.org/country-detail/rwanda/
- The National Land Authority and MININFRA describe land-use, planning and geospatial information services. Public information means Taiseel must add verified workflow and decision value rather than claim a moat from map access alone: https://www.lands.rw/land-use-and-mapping and https://www.mininfra.gov.rw/1/urbanisation-human-settlement-and-housing-development
- Existing property and land discovery products include Baza, Tura, PropSpera, IkazeProperty and NIHEHE's parcel lookup. A generic listings interface is therefore not a sufficient differentiator: https://baza.rw/ · https://tura.rw/ · https://www.propspera.rw/ · https://www.ikazeproperty.rw/en · https://parcels.nihehe.rw/
